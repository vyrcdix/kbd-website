Support for the parents who love them

Most parents arrive worried they’ve done something wrong, and they haven’t. Some of the most useful work I do never has a child in the room at all.

Parent sessions
A place to think out loud about your child with someone who has sat with a few hundred of them. Sometimes it’s all a family needs, and often it’s where we begin.
Circle of Security®
An attachment-based approach to parenting that I’m trained to facilitate: less technique, and more understanding of what your child is asking for underneath the behaviour.
Neurodivergent families
AutPlay® certified and neurodiversity-affirming. If you’re exhausted by systems and wary of anyone talking about fixing your child, we’ll get along.
Families in transition
Separation, blending, fostering, adopting, or a loss the whole household is carrying. Both homes are welcome to be part of the work.

You won’t be handed a programme

I won’t send you home with a behaviour chart or a schedule to run. We’ll talk about what’s actually happening in your house, what your child seems to be telling you, and what would help this week. You’ll always know what’s emerging in your child’s sessions.

Reading first is fine too

The BC Play Therapy Association publishes a caregivers’ guide to finding a play therapist, including the questions worth asking any therapist you’re considering, me included. It isn’t me selling myself, which is exactly why it’s useful.

Caregivers’ guide (BCPTA, PDF)

Most people write two lines, which is plenty, and we can work the rest out together. If you’d rather talk first, we can set up a free 15-minute call with no obligation and no paperwork.
